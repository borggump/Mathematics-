Room-Temperature Polariton Supersolid

The mathematics behind the University of Warsaw result, with interactive diagrams

The article describes a supersolid: a state of matter that is simultaneously a superfluid (flows with zero viscosity) and a crystal-like density wave (its density repeats periodically in space). Achieving this with light-matter hybrid particles at about 60 °C is the key result. Below, each piece is built up from first principles, then the experimental signatures are derived from the same equations.

1. Exciton polaritons: the particles

An exciton is a bound electron-hole pair in a semiconductor. When it is strongly coupled to a photon trapped in a microcavity (two mirrors a fraction of a micron apart), the eigenstates are mixtures of light and matter. These are polaritons, with a lower-branch dispersion that is approximately

ELP(k) ≈ Ex + ħ²k² / (2m*),    m* ≈ 10−5–10−4 me (1)

The effective mass m* is tiny because the particle is mostly photon. Two consequences follow. First, the condensation temperature scales as Tc ∝ n/m*, so a light mass lets condensation happen far above the nanokelvin regime needed for cold atoms. Second, the particle is lossy. Its lifetime is set by the cavity and exciton decay rate γ, and the article notes it is only a few picoseconds.

Scale check. The article's 60 °C is T = 333 K, so kBT ≈ 8.617×10−5 eV/K × 333 K ≈ 28.7 meV. For a 1 ps lifetime, the energy linewidth is ħ/τ = (6.58×10−16 eV·s)/(10−12 s) ≈ 0.66 meV, so the thermal energy is roughly 40 times the linewidth. The condensate is therefore only possible because the mass and density conditions make the coherent state dominate.

2. The order parameter and the driven-dissipative Gross-Pitaevskii equation

A condensate of many bosons is described by one complex macroscopic wavefunction. Write it in amplitude-phase form:

ψ(r,t) = √n(r,t) · eiφ(r,t) (2)

Here n is the density and φ is the phase. Because polaritons are pumped by a laser and leak out of the cavity, the dynamics are driven and dissipative. The standard equation is

iħ ∂ψ/∂t = [ −(ħ²/2m*)∇² + V(r) + g|ψ|² + (iħ/2)(R·nR − γ) ] ψ (3)
  • g is the polariton-polariton interaction strength. It is repulsive and is what makes the condensate stiff.
  • V(r) is an external potential, which in the experiment is set by the cavity structure and the electrically tunable liquid crystal.
  • R·nR is gain from the pump and reservoir. γ is loss. A steady state needs gain and loss to balance.

In the absence of gain and loss, eq. (3) conserves total particle number, which gives the continuity equation below.

∂n/∂t + ∇·(n v) = 0,    v = (ħ/m*) ∇φ (4)

The superfluid velocity is the phase gradient. This is the first clue for superfluidity: flow is encoded entirely in how the phase varies in space.

3. Superfluidity: frictionless flow

3a. Bogoliubov excitations and the critical velocity

Linearize eq. (3) around a uniform condensate of density n₀ and let εp = p²/2m*. The collective excitations have the Bogoliubov dispersion

E(p) = √( εp (εp + 2g n₀) ) (5)

For small momenta, E(p) ≈ c·p with sound speed c = √(g n₀ / m*). Landau's criterion says a fluid moving at speed v cannot lose energy to excitations unless some excitation can be created with E(p) < p·v. The fluid therefore flows without friction as long as its speed stays below

vc = minp [ E(p)/p ] = √( g n₀ / m* ) (6)

This is the mathematical version of the article's oar analogy. Below vc, the oar meets no resistance. Above it, quantum vortices nucleate. The interactive plot in section 6 shows E(p)/p and its minimum.

3b. Quantized vortices

Because ψ must be single-valued, circulation around a closed loop is quantized:

∮ v · dl = (h / m*) · N,   N ∈ ℤ (7)

Vortices are the topological defects that let a superfluid respond to rotation or to an oar above threshold, which is exactly what the article describes.

4. The supersolid: density wave plus superflow

Now allow the density to be periodic. A minimal ansatz with a density modulation of wavenumber K and relative amplitude ε is

n(x) = n̄ [ 1 + ε cos(Kx − θ) ],   φ(x) = const.  (superflow still allowed) (8)

The modulation's period is Λ = 2π/K. The superfluid has to carry flow while also having a periodic density, and these two things compete. A supersolid exists when a sizable fraction of the condensate still moves without friction despite the crystal-like modulation. Quantitatively, one writes the superfluid fraction ρs/ρ, which is strictly between 0 and 1 in a supersolid.

4a. Spontaneous symmetry breaking

The energy functional is invariant under a continuous translation of the whole pattern, because nothing in the Hamiltonian picks out where the fringes sit. Mathematically, the free parameter θ in eq. (8) can take any value in [0, 2π). Every choice gives the same energy:

E[n(x); θ] = E[n(x); θ + α]  for all α ∈ ℝ   ⇒   a continuous family of degenerate ground states (9)

The system spontaneously picks one θ. This is spontaneous breaking of continuous translation symmetry, the defining property of a crystal. Each pulse of the laser starts a new realization, and the fringes land at a new position:

Δx = Δθ / K = (Λ / 2π) · Δθ (10)

This is the article's "bright fringes appear in a different position each time." The measured fringe shift is a direct readout of the random choice of θ.

5. Persistent spin helix and polarization

5a. Two-component (spin) description

Polaritons carry a pseudospin given by their circular polarization, so the condensate has two components: ψ↑ (right circular) and ψ↓ (left circular). The polarization of the emitted light is described by the Stokes parameters:

S0 = |ψ↑|² + |ψ↓|²,   S1 = |ψ↑|² − |ψ↓|²,   S2 = 2 Re(ψ↑* ψ↓),   S3 = 2 Im(ψ↑* ψ↓) (11)

S0 is the intensity. The polarization orientation is set by (S1, S2), and the linear polarization angle is α = ½ arctan(S2/S1).

5b. Spin-orbit coupling and the helix

In a microcavity with a liquid crystal layer, the TE and TM photon modes split. The effective spin-orbit Hamiltonian for the polariton pseudospin is a momentum-dependent effective magnetic field Ω(k):

HSO = ½ Ω(k) · σ,   Ω(k) = (βxkx, βyky) (schematically) (12)

When the two spin-orbit strengths are equal, the spin precesses as one moves along a line. The spin texture is then a helix, with the pseudospin angle winding linearly:

φs(x) = Q x + φ0,   Q ∝ m*β / ħ² (13)

The linear polarization angle therefore rotates with x, and S1, S2 oscillate as cos(Qx), sin(Qx). This is the article's "persistent spin helix": the polarization pattern is periodic in space and, in the ideal model, does not decay along the helix.

5c. Why two orders coexist

Eq. (8) gives the intensity order (fringes in S0). Eq. (13) gives the polarization order (a rotating angle in S1, S2). The supersolid signature is that both appear in the same state, which is why the paper reports "two different forms of order occurring at the same time."
